### Selected problem

MSU-IIT students, staff, and visitors spend time wandering across college buildings searching for different offices because indoor floor plans are not mapped in standard navigation maps, leading to unnecessary delays when doing tasks.

### Selection rationale

We chose this as the problem to be solved because digitizing campus navigation solves a practical issue that many students, organization officers, and visitors who are unfamiliar with campus grounds often face when locating administrative desks and faculty offices across multiple campus buildings. We have easy access to stakeholders since we can interview students and office staff on campus. The canteen problem was not chosen because of the reliance on e-wallet integration and vendor cooperation, which are hard to implement within our timeline. Likewise, the smoke and haze dashboard idea was the most difficult of the three. It was considered too technical since handling high-volume data payloads and cleaning and normalizing them would be a massive bottleneck in development.

## 3\. Preliminary Project Proposal

### Working project title

**iitmaps**: every office, one search away 

### Problem statement

MSU-IIT students, especially freshmen and organization officers, along with visitors and researchers, often need to locate faculty offices, departments, and administrative desks spread across colleges. Standard navigation apps map only outdoor roads, and the floor plans posted in some colleges are obscure. Currently, people ask security guards (or anyone nearby) or walk the corridors until they find the right room. This costs time and causes frustration, especially for urgent submissions. We want a digital map with indoor floor plans that lets users search for an office and see where it is. We assume that most users would prefer a mobile-friendly tool over asking for help, that existing campus maps can serve as a starting layout, and that office locations stay stable enough to keep the data current. These assumptions require validation through surveys and interviews. 

### Proposed solution

We propose a web-based interactive spatial directory for MSU-IIT that digitizes existing campus and floor maps into a searchable, mobile-friendly platform. Users may type the name of an office, department, or administrative desk and instantly see its building, floor, and room highlighted on the floor plan. We will start with two pilot buildings, CASS and CCS, using existing campus maps as the starting layout and verifying room locations through on-site visits. We will survey and interview students, organization officers, and visitors to test whether the tool is helpful and preferred over seeking help. By replacing static posted floor plans and asking around for instant, searchable answers, the directory helps users reach the right office faster, especially when they have urgent tasks to do.

## 5\. Initial Scope

### Intended users and setting

Students and other people who need directions at MSU-IIT are our intended users for this project. When they want to locate a building, office, or a particular room on campus for whatever purpose, our project will provide them with the information they need, saving time and energy compared to asking for directions and unclear wayfinding.

### In-scope capabilities

1. Search for a faculty office, department office, or administrative desk by name.  
2. View the resulting building, floor, and room highlighted on a digital floor plan.  
3. Switch between floors and buildings.  
4. Browse or filter offices by building and floor.

### Out-of-scope capabilities

1. Indoor map data other than CASS and CCS.  
2. Live indoor GPS tracking.  
3. Turn-by-turn navigation and routing between rooms. 

### Expected deliverables

1. A working prototype of iitmaps with search, floor plan view, and floor and building switching for CASS and CCS.  
2. A digitized dataset of floor plans and office listings for CASS and CCS, with a verified date for each entry.  
3. Project documentation, including this proposal, a short user guide, and setup notes for running the prototype.  
4. Testing evidence, including survey responses, interview notes, and usability test records.  
5. A source code repository with the final prototype.

### Constraints

| Constraint | Effect on the project | How the team will respond |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Limited time in one semester | Only a few buildings and core features can be built | Limit the scope to two pilot buildings, plus search and floor view |
| Room data must be collected and checked manually | Slow work, and listings may be wrong if offices have moved | Use existing campus maps as a base, verify through on-site visits, and record the date each listing was last checked |
| Limited access to official floor plans | The base layouts may be incomplete | Request plans from the administration, and sketch from on-site measurements if unavailable |

### Assumptions and dependencies

## 6\. Review and Next Steps

### Changes made after reviewing the proposal

- Before review: The directory would cover offices across several colleges, including CCS, CASS, CSM, and COE.   
- After review: The first version covers only two pilot buildings, CASS and CCS. Other buildings are out of scope for this semester.   
- Reason: Mapping every college would require verifying many rooms within one semester. Two pilot buildings keep the data accurate and let us test the idea with real users before expanding. 

### Biggest remaining risk

Our dependence on accurate floor plan data. We do not yet know whether usable floor plans of CASS and CCS exist, and manual mapping may be slow or contain errors. Offices may also move, which would make listings wrong and lose users’ trust. If the data is unreliable, the directory could send people to the wrong room, which is worse than the current approach. We will reduce this by verifying a sample of rooms through on-site visits and recording a last-verified date for each listing.

### Three unanswered questions

1. Can we obtain official or more detailed floor plans for CASS and CCS from the colleges or administration?  
2. Will users actually prefer the directory over asking someone for help, especially when they have urgent submissions?  
3. How long will it take our team to collect and verify the room data for every floor of CASS and CCS, and can this be finished early enough to leave time for building and testing the prototype?
